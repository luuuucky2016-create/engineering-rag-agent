# Electrical Basics — Quick Reference

## Ohm's law
Voltage, current and resistance are related by V = I × R, where V is in volts (V), I in amperes (A) and R in ohms (Ω). Rearranged: I = V / R and R = V / I.

## Power in DC circuits
P = V × I = I² × R = V² / R, with P in watts (W).

## Power in AC circuits
- Single-phase real power: P = V × I × PF, where PF is the power factor (0 to 1).
- Three-phase real power (balanced load): P = √3 × V_L × I_L × PF, where V_L is the line-to-line voltage and I_L the line current.
- Apparent power S (VA) = V × I for single-phase, or √3 × V_L × I_L for three-phase. Real power P = S × PF.

## Finding current from a known load
- Single-phase: I = P / (V × PF)
- Three-phase: I = P / (√3 × V_L × PF)
Example: a 15 kW three-phase motor at 400 V with PF 0.85 draws I = 15000 / (1.732 × 400 × 0.85) ≈ 25.5 A.

## Low-voltage supply in Saudi Arabia
The standard low-voltage supply is 230/400 V at 60 Hz (230 V phase-to-neutral, 400 V line-to-line).
